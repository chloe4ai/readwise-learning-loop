Learning Loop

Readwise closes the loop on what you read. More and more of what people learn now happens in conversations with AI — and none of it comes back for review. This prototype tries three ideas:

  1. Capture: turn Claude / ChatGPT conversations into highlights worth remembering.
  2. Review: make Daily Review aware of what you're working on this week.
  3. Read next new · for Readwise Books: let your highlights, and what you've been learning in AI chats, pick your next book.

How it works

Capture

  1. Parse. Claude's and ChatGPT's conversations.json exports, or a pasted transcript, are normalized into turns. For ChatGPT, only the branch you kept (walking up from current_node) is used — regenerated answers you abandoned are ignored.
  2. Score sentences. Each sentence of each answer gets transparent signals: definitions, contrasts ("not X but Y"), rules of thumb, causal claims, bold emphasis, whether it answered your question, and — the strongest signal — whether your next message reacted ("oh interesting", "didn't know that", "原来如此"). Filler ("Great question!") and sentences that depend on context ("It matters most when…") are dropped.
  3. Pick a few. At most two per answer and four per conversation, with near-duplicates suppressed. Your question becomes the highlight's note, so the review card has context.
  4. Send. Highlights go to Readwise via POST /api/v2/highlights/ (or a CSV import) with source_type=learning_loop and .ai-chat tags.

Context-aware review

final = w · relevance(context) + (1 − w) · dueness, where relevance is BM25 over highlight text, title and tags, and dueness is days since last review ÷ current interval (capped at 1). The list is then re-ranked with Maximal Marginal Relevance so one book can't take over the session. Chinese text is tokenized with character bigrams, so it works without a segmenter.

This is the same shape as an ad or feed ranking stack — a relevance model, a business constraint (spacing), and a diversity pass — scaled down to one reader.

What to read next (Readwise Books)

final = wtaste · taste + wintent · intent + wgap · gap, then MMR over topics.

  • Taste: BM25 affinity between each of your highlights and each book, averaged over the three strongest highlights. Those three are kept as the explanation (“because you highlighted…”).
  • Intent: how well the book fits what you typed for this week, in English or Chinese.
  • Gap: the share of that evidence that came from AI conversations. If most of what you know about auctions came from Claude, a real book on auctions is the natural next step.
  • Already read: books you already have highlights from (matched on title or translated title, plus author) are removed instead of being sold to you again.

This is the commerce version of the same ranking shape: a home feed that blends long-term affinity with session intent, keeps one category from taking over, and says why every item is there.

What a production version would change

  • Embeddings instead of BM25 for relevance (Readwise already built hybrid search for MCP).
  • Context inferred, not typed: what's in your Reader inbox this week, your recent tags, your calendar.
  • An evaluation loop: does context-aware review raise "Keep" / "Mastery" rates and 30-day retention versus control?
  • For Books: rank the real catalog with embeddings, add reading-session signals (finished, abandoned, re-read), and test the "for you" shelf against bestsellers on sample opens, purchases and books finished.
